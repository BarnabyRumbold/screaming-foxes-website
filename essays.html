The difficulty of writing Playing Games

Hey, this is my first of these. I thought it would be cool to… share how I have been thinking and what has been happening while I have been writing these songs. Things are coming together on the marketing side, and I guess I can’t really put this off any longer. I am not sure why I have been putting it off, I journal regularly and this is, in essence, the same thing. But I think it’s because, if I am honest, I didn’t find making this album and these songs that much fun really and it isn’t something I look back on fondly. It felt like a purge, like a stream of consciousness or a tearful release during therapy. Needed or cathartic maybe, but not comfortable.

Playing Games was the first song that I wrote, and I wrote and rewrote it 37 times. I had the first two thirds, cannibalised from a song from an old band and then had this outro with these picked chords that I really loved, with these lyrics that weren’t really about anything but just came out in the moment. Then the first part of the song felt wrong. Rushed and… cold and flat. Especially compared to the more honest, last part of the song.

I think the difference was that the outro sounded like how I felt, it didn’t sound like what I wanted to sound like. And even though it maybe wasn’t as cool as I wanted to be, it was real and there was a little spark in that. I kept working on that, over and over, until the outro had been binned and nothing of the original song remained, and I had 37 versions of the songs on my Google Drive. I realised eventually that what I was trying to say, is that I had just been wasting my time, playing games with people that don’t go anywhere, and that it felt great to… not be doing that anymore. Sad to have done it, but relief to be free from it. To have the space and time to be bored and peaceful. Hence the lyrics.

Singing it after this, tracking it, recording it, mixing it, getting the movement of it right, just came together. This was the hardest I had ever worked on a song, and it showed. It had a feeling and ambience, and it wasn’t particularly cool, at least to my ears, but at least it was honest and that felt like it was worth something. Maybe no one ever hears it, but it felt good to both refine something until it lands and to start to be properly honest through music.
